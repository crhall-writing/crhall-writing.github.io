Gil’s in Paris with a fiancee who isn’t portrayed to be a demon    
1. painting Inez as the devil reveals an intense character flaw in Gil that makes him incredibly unlikeable and totally void of relatability from anyone who isn’t a total hack—why would he ever ask someone so materialistic, so selfish, so unsupportive, for her hand in the first place? is it because he can brag to other men that she’s hot? surely that’s how Woody picks and chooses his partners—his step daughter was 21 and he was 56 when they went public with their relationship   
2. painting Inez as the devil removes any interesting moral, emotional, or logistical element to Gil eventually leaving her. he does not leave her because he is taking control of his life—he is leaving her because she is nothing more than a pretty face to him, and at his ripe age of 35-40 he realizes something others learn at age 19; in this, he is not portrayed to be juvenile or idiotic for coming to such a basic, obvious conclusion at such an old age—instead, people—specifically men also lacking this realization—are taught that men like Gil deserve a pat on the back for realizing love should be more than a way to brag to other men about how sexy your fiancee is  
3. painting Inez as the devil removes Woody’s responsibility to develop an interesting portrayal of the aftermath of leaving someone who loved you due to reasons greater than a marriage could have been; this is evidently nothing Woody has ever experienced, leaving his relationship with an of-age woman to pursue his step daughter, not because he was “taking back his life”; in fact, I’d argue that Woody left a full life for a much shallower existence, solely to pursue one-dimensionality, something I’m sure he’d never want the male audience of Midnight in Paris to notice    
4. painting Inez as the devil removes the realization all men playing with the idea of cheating on their partners should come to—Gil is absolved of his guilt because he found out—long after he had stolen his fiancee’s earrings to be more able to sleep with other women, long after he had kissed other women, long after he had desperately came onto someone who didn’t initiate interest in him, while he was actively engaged—that his own fiancee had been cheating on him as well; Woody portrays this as totally acceptable, that Gil is innocent, and Inez is guilty, and moreso Inez’s guilt actively absolves Gil from self reflection, remorse, or self improvement; I believe many men who feel the same inclinations to window shop will see this double-standard as reasonable since Inez and Paul are directed by Woody to be annoying; I think the actual reason is because Gil is a man and Inez is a woman—insufferable and selfish men are “human” and “figuring it out” in this universe, the value of a woman, meanwhile, is placed on how agreeable, fun, or inspiring she is—if she fails to turn a man into a great writer with her own bare hands—or, thinking in Woody’s perspective, bare breasts—she is not only useless, but also a villain   
5. painting Inez as the devil should—if this script were written by someone more intent on appealing to brains rather than swollen, tiny dicks—imply a redemption route that should wind through hell, maybe even hell and back—instead it’s to tell essentially a man child that “yes, it’s totally possible to become a world famous novelist without ever showing the novel to anyone, and I am so wrong for suggesting you do so” or “I should really start acting more like a 17 year old you just met—I know that would light the fire in your pants that I am solely responsible for killing due to being an adult and not a child” or “wow I have just decided that walking in the rain is so much more important than being a decent human being! you were so right for cheating on me with someone because they thought waling in the rain sounded fun when i didn’t that particular day as I am trying to plan a wedding you no-doubtedly are not involved in or helping with at all because you’re too busy running around finding women to cheat with”. however, I think if this character were written with any type of vigor enough to justify the actions of Gil, her walking the path of redemption through hell would reveal Woody is already there—hopefully in some secluded corner so that the actual devil doesn’t have to put up with Woody’s putrid stench on his own morning strolls   
6. Adrianna should be axed as a character completely—more on this later, but if Woody really, really needed to keep her in so he could ejaculate his shriveled up dick when overdosing on Viagra has clearly stopped working, this would’ve been a perfect chance to show Gil that he uses almost everything in life, not just eras but people, as excuses for his own mediocrity and complacency. his attempt to latch onto Adrianna, desperate to cheat on his wife for being… hot but not publishing his novel for him… could’ve shown Gil that thinking the woman he is with will make or break his success as a writer is also just as infantile as his thought that a read out on the calendar will transform him from mediocre and lazy to inspired and great. Woody, evidently, has lived his entire life hopping towards younger women, so I can’t expect such a waste of breath to write such a conclusion since he has yet to reach it, even into his geriatric years, himself    

the woman Gil ends up with in the end is essentially fluff—fat needed to be cut off of an empty plot line; she doesn’t exist in a correct version of this script  
1. this character acts as nothing more than a distraction from what should be the entire point of the movie—that nothing—a different era, differently colored street lights, a different clothing style—will ever make up for a willingness to grab life by the horns and birth your own path; that art and value must be constructed anew from smoking, lively embers and not from stale ashes; instead, Woody—being the clever guy he is—finds a way to totally miss his own point and imply that sure, a different era won’t save you, but a different woman—a younger and hotter one preferably, and one that… likes to walk in the rain? wow how special, it’s not like anyone’s ever wanted to do that before…—will  

  
Gertrude Stein is a hollowed representation of what she was, or could’ve been in this universe—a meaningful story would make her a critic, and not an empty enabler   
1. she acts as nothing more than a device to not provide Gil any type of real mentorship, critique, or development, but instead as a mirror to what Gil wants to already think about himself—that he is naturally gifted, perfect, and that Inez is the devil because she’s… just hot but not giving him immediate success as something he has not even tried at himself. Oh, wait, I didn’t mean to give the discharge coming out the ears of Woody’s swollen and throbbing tumor of a head, I meant to say, in Woody’s words “she’s having an affair… forget though that Gil was already doing that though, of course”   
2. treating Gil as immediately one of the greats—comparable to Hemingway and Fitzgerald and Joyce, on the first external read of his first novel, is incredibly idiotic and lacking in intrigue. you’re telling me Gil being perfect in all ways from the get go, while still being an Adam-Sandler-Stereotype imbecile, and his desire to walk in the rain doing all the heavy work of great and thoughtful living and writing, is better, more engaging, than Stein telling him his novel just isn’t that good, and him having to come to the realization that thinking romantically and hoping everyone in the world will bend to his flimsy will has nothing to do with great work? furthermore, there was nothing interesting that could be gathered about the time period in which these greats were living—that they themselves were young writers who had not become 100-year-old greats or written into literature courses, that they also felt the uncertainty of surviving as a writer in the post-war era? oh whoops, Woody didn’t have enough time of that, since he was too busy writing about his cheating fantasies instead of thinking about writing an interesting script  
  

every literary giant introduced should not be some flat, empty, comical caricature that somehow are so basic they aren’t likable, and yet so hidden and romanticized, any flaws that could make the story interesting is completely erased   
1. Zelda, of course, is allowed to be flawed because she is a woman. classic Woody—we already know his ability to write is a waste of neurons. however, Zelda’s flaws in and of themselves are chosen in such an inaccurate, lazy, and “essay started at 10pm and due at midnight” manner, that I’m unsure if Woody’s research was done by a team or the worms that inhabit the tiny brain bobbing around in his gargantuan, thick head. she is nothing more than a drunk, unstable, suicidal, insecure muse for the “great” Scott. in reality, Zelda actually provided material and ideas stolen by Fitzgerald, used in work under his own name; Scott actually became angry at Zelda for drawing inspiration from their relationship in her own writing, claiming it was “his” territory to push his own name at the expense of hers  
2. Zelda is portrayed—of course, because she is a woman—as the unstable witch/siren/what-have-you of the Fitzgerald marriage, and fails to showcase the devastating, earth-shattering instability and alcoholism Scott himself had. there is no inclusion of one, singular, actually interesting reality or question provoked by Woody, since Woody, like Coco the Gorilla, has yet to develop the necessary mental faculties to ask a question: for all the men who are seen to be “Great Men”, what did it cost everyone around them—how did their alcoholism, infidelity, abuse, absentee parenthood, due to the rest of those who actually knew them, instead of asking how hard they made the microscopic penis of Woody?   
3. it’s somewhat understandable to portray infidelity in media without needing a fourth-wall break to tell the viewer, “this is bad to do”. but in a movie so obsessed with exploring infidelity, surely to quell the consciousness of sir Woody the Grandest of Cheaters himself, Woody sure does manage to completely bulldoze over one of the most interesting aspects of Hemingway’s life: that he cheated on his wife with her best friend, when the three of them would hang out as a group all the time. in fact, the wife gave Hemingway her full blessing to go after her friend, telling him so long as he spent 100 days apart from the friend, and still was just as madly in love with the idea of her after that period, she would give them her blessing without question. Hemingway, even with the understanding of a village about his selfish betrayal of everyone around him, agreed to this deal, yet behind his wife’s back sent letters to her friend the entire waiting period, and still went on to marry the friend anyway. again, Woody had all the capacity in the world to ask such an obvious, interesting question: who paid for the glory of these “Great Men” that Woody thinks about sodomizing every night? instead, we get a difficult-to-watch hallucination of Hemingway as nothing more than a steak-eating, whiskey-drinking… guy… with nothing interesting to say, only to—with the visual of a thick mustache—evoke in men, men who wish to see themselves as literary romantics, geniuses, next-generation thinkers, a shadow of a thought about the name of a man they have come to associate vaguely with historical importance    
4. Woody misses opportunities to address the exact same themes, morals, or whatever he was desperately trying to show off with in this script, using the life of literature instead of villainous women; Adrianna never had to exist to show him people of that day harped on the past just as much as Gil—Woody is so obsessed with maintaining the perfection of men, and putting imperfection, naivety, and unreasonableness upon women and women alone, that he completely ignores an opportunity to make himself look like anything but a fraud for being able to sell this script in the first place. how no one on his team screamed at him, “no, you idiot! the entire plot of The Great Gatsby is so obsessed with nostalgia and ignoring current life, reaching for an idealized past with Daisy, that one could practically say you stole Gatsby’s plot—butchered and maimed it into a stupid movie with no substance whatsoever—and you, even then, are too dumb to have read Gatsby in the first place to see that!” Scott would immediately tell Gil exactly what Adrianna had about escaping to the past, but this reveals a two-fold problem: Woody is too inflated to even perform basic research on those he wishes to portray as buzz names to hype himself up as a literature buff without ever having read a book, and this realization coming from the flawed perspective of a Man would force Woody to actually portray any type of depth and complexity about Great Men—something he is far too much of an imbecile to do    
5. in fact, Woody is so dense we never even hear the term “Lost Generation”—the entire point of these jerkoffs living in Paris is to make a statement about how the pre-war era was too glamorous and they are desperately chasing an era they no longer exist in—that’s the whole reason they left America. well, I suppose it’s still unclear whether leaving America was due more to this or the absolute desperation to get drunk on the daily and fuel their individual alcoholisms due to Prohibition in the States  

  
my overall takeaway: it is impressive to be so stupid and immoral as a person, you can miss the point of your own story—for instance, Woody, amazingly, achieves all three:  
1. telling the audience that a different era will not give Gil the things he must do himself in life, but another woman will   
2. condemning nostalgia while fully inaccurately portraying the past in such a carefully wrong way to preserve as much nostalgia as possible  
3. the Lost Generation, and all historical context surrounding them—their hardships, mental illnesses, abuse to others around them—is used as nothing more than a flashy backdrop to provide a aesthetics that will make a movie more sellable; it’s almost as if the real empty-headed, empty-souled screenwriter was never Gil, but instead Woody
