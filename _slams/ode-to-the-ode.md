I want to write an ode to the women of long island   
just kidding  

I want to perform, actually, an ode to the “ode to the women of long island”  

if you don’t know it, that’s okay  

the great poet Olivia Gatwood performed at the Button slam back when that was still a thing    
flash to October, 2016, I was new to the city, one back pocket filled with an iPhone 3 and I wish I could say the other kept some type of dream.   
the front pockets were always left to keep my fingers because I never knew what to do with my hands    

and on that note, I was riding over on the train for my second date, with some guy studying theater and computer science and on our first date he made me play Go to show off his strategic apparatus, and like an abacus I kept track of groups of tens of resume-worthy life events, making sure to carry the one each time he added on enough to the list  

and on that train he asked if I was OK, I was quiet, so quiet, he said, that he wondered if I shared his same brain, that we go silent thinking of the way every single person we see has their own full and characterized life, regardless if we ever hear them tell their story.   

I said sure, surely, but I had that grand epiphany at age 11 already and really the reason that I was so quiet, was that I wondered if anyone else noticed I was forming pit stains through my shirt.   
I figured out a good deodorant since then    

and then it happened. Miss Gatwood performed her Ode, said the iconic line, “I will make a hero out of you” like the sandwich, and I thought man can this bitch command a room   

and I’ve been using my time playing Go with this fucking guy who seemed to only like me for how much he could see his own reflection in my shiny ass forehead   
(I figured out skincare since then)  

and, believe it or not, I lived on Long Island for five years, and I spent the majority of my time there breaking up male centric circle jerks and chopping it up with the fucking Women of Long Island, who deserve every ode that could ever be written about them  

young and shy me was adopted by new mothers, these mothers had the guts and courage to leave when their husbands would cheat, when my own mother would tell me to shut my mouth and make it work without a peep when what happened to her started happening to me    

and these Long Island mothers would read my dreams, in the same vain, just as throbbing as those on the hard dicks that long island men get for dostoyevsky or rand or camus or david foster wallace, I would no longer fall in love with anything but long pink acrylics, these queens would interpret my tarot cards to show hierophants in reverse, towers to crumble, the same way a long island man’s ego falls the moment he’s forced to bow down to whoever is the tallest and loudest in the room   

and I returned to Minneapolis, from Long Island, completely anew, and now I am the loudest in the room  

and just as Miss. Legend, Miss Mam, Miss Gatwood herself looked me in the eye and said   

they will call you a bitch  

they called me a bitch even as I screamed and kicked my way to this very spot, where I know exactly what to do with my hands and to stand as tall as the tales of how I walk   

from two thousand miles they call me a bitch  

and I look over my shoulder and I say thank you, thank you very much
