---
title: the octopus
date: 2026-08-30
layout: poem
---

I am an octopus  
maybe I was never an arthropod to anyone in this audience  
but to someone I had been, for far too long  

I have eight arms somehow, not just two  
to embrace you when you feel you’re not enough  

I have four billion neurons waiting at the ready  
to find a way to communicate all the thoughts inside my brain  
as slowly, carefully, controlledly as a bomb technician  

and my mission seems like that of an octopus  
judged by the eyes of anthropologists  
AKA, apologists to the human experience  
who judge anything that moves through their egocentric lenses  
as hominids, nothing more, and nothing less  

I am an octopus  
though our nearest relative lived  
750 million years past  
the story we both have come to tell  
is one of survival  

when others want to judge how well we recognize our reflection  
mine inflates and deflates at the will of another  
I am either 115 pounds or 155  
and I actually have not a single inkling, not a single clue  
but for some reason, that feels important to know  
when I’m around you  

when survival wants something that you never will  
resilience, confidence, a sheer force instilled  
to crack open shells for nutrients  
to kill when we need to kill  

but listen to this  
did you know octopuses punch fish out of spite?  
and scientists look on that, and don’t see something flawed  
they see them fascinating as opposed to the other, gentler creatures  
which float in the sea, giving nothing of substance to write papers about  

I am an octopus  
capable of changing my colors to not only show the state of my nervous system  
but when turning deep red is no longer convenient, or puts me in danger  
I can blend, though myriads of speckles and gradients, into my surroundings  
I can abandon my meridian, that is, if I ever had one  
and you or him or him or him can pass as freely as you’d think I should let it happen  

scientists say one of the greatest paradoxes of the octopus  
is that it contains the faculties to express the most complex language in the animal kingdom  
an infinite number of ways to express cooperation and motive and experience and reaction  
unlike apes who carry only a small number of grunts with large dependence on surrounding context  
it’s rare to see a group of octopuses hanging out  
without it turning into the squishy, tentacl-y WWE  
they are some of the most antisocial organisms  
and in fact their amazing capabilities of communication come from their strict need to tell others to
back off  

and that leads me to the most interesting fact  
even with all of that  
their average lifespan is 3-5 years for the big ones, 1-2 years for the small ones  
they actually begin to self destruct, disintegrating from the inside,  
their flesh eventually picked apart by scavengers while they’re still barely alive  
immediately after producing their offspring  
the octopus, one can say, lives only to reproduce and die  
and yet we pay its short life, and all of its story, so much value, and so much glory  
that our concept of its longevity far outweighs its reality  

I have already lived much longer than the octopus  
when will my life mean more than repopulating myself and dissolving from the inside out?
